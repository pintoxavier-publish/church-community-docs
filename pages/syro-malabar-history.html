Syro-Malabar Church: A Living Legacy

The Syro-Malabar Church is an autonomous (sui iuris) Eastern Catholic Church based primarily in Kerala, India. It traces its spiritual origins to St. Thomas the Apostle, who landed at Muziris (Kodungallur) in AD 52, making its early members part of the historic St. Thomas Christian (Nazrani) community.

Ancient Roots and East Syrian Connection

Apostolic Beginnings

St. Thomas founded seven early Christian communities in Kerala (including Palayur, Paravur, and Quilon) before dying a martyr in Mylapore (Chennai) in AD 72.

Syriac Tradition

Over the centuries, the community maintained close ecclesiastical ties with the East Syrian (Chaldean) Church, adopting its liturgical and spiritual traditions while retaining an indigenous Indian culture.

Portuguese Era and the Coonan Cross Oath

Latinization

When Portuguese colonizers arrived in the 16th century, they imposed Latin customs and jurisdiction, disrupting the community's traditional East Syriac heritage.

The Schism

Resenting these changes, a large segment of the community revolted in 1653 via the historic Coonan Cross Oath. This event split the St. Thomas Christians into those who aligned with Western Latin structures (forming the roots of the Syro-Malabar Catholic Church) and those who eventually aligned with West Syrian/Jacobite traditions.

Modern Autonomy and Hierarchy

Name and Separation

The name "Syro-Malabar" came into official Roman use in the 19th century to denote the Syrian Christians of the Malabar Coast in communion with Rome.

Indigenous Bishops

Pope Leo XIII established separate vicariates and appointed local native bishops for the community in 1896.

Full Hierarchy

The official Syro-Malabar hierarchy was organized in 1923 under Archbishop Augustine Kandathil. In 1992, Pope John Paul II elevated the church to a Major Archiepiscopal Church.

Present Day

Today, the Syro-Malabar Church is the second-largest Eastern Catholic Church globally, boasting millions of faithful both in Kerala and spread across a worldwide diaspora.
